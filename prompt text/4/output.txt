v1.15 is live on the site and in the repo for the iPhone app. Everything you listed is in, on both platforms, with one thing you must do in Firebase (below) before the teacher side works.

## Lists

**Lists** (a header button on the site; Words → **Lists** on the phone) shows every list with its word count. Rename it and every word follows, along with the deck and the "add new words to" setting; create an empty one; delete one and choose *keep the words* (they become ungrouped) or *delete the words too*; **Use** makes it the list new words go to. Lists you create stay even while empty. Any list is still a deck in Review.

## Dates on the backend

Every word has carried the moment it was added since v1.6 (`t`, milliseconds, synced with the word; the account document also gets a server-side `updated` stamp on each save). What's new is that the date is now used: it orders the Saved tab, it drives the Added filter in Review, and words a teacher sends are stamped with the time they were sent plus the teacher's email (`by`), so you can see where a word came from.

## Saved

A **Saved** tab now sits at the top of the rail (Words → Saved on the phone): everything you actually added — from the dictionary, a JSON file, a set or a teacher — across all lists, newest first, each row showing its list and, for teacher-sent words, who sent it. The 270-word starter file every new copy gets is left out. The site opens on this tab once there's anything in it.

## Teachers

Any account can be a teacher; there's no separate sign-up.

1. **Teaching → Create my teacher code** gives you a 7-character code (same alphabet as set codes).
2. A student opens **Teaching → My teachers**, enters the code, and the link is made. Only the student can create that link, and either side can remove it. A teacher can have any number of students; a student any number of teachers.
3. The teacher's Teaching page lists each student with their word count, last save and last review. **Open** shows their whole file by list, with a **Remove** on every word, and an **Add words** box: search the dictionary, collect words, name the list they go into (default "From <your email>"), **Send to student**.
4. The change lands in the student's file the moment their app is open — website or phone — filed under the list you named, stamped with the time and your email, and announced to them ("teacher@… added 12 words to “Week 3”"). Until then it shows under *Waiting for their app to open*, where you can withdraw it.

The teacher never writes the student's file directly: changes go through an inbox the student's own app applies. That's deliberate — it means a teacher adding words while the student is mid-session can't overwrite anything, and the student's device remains the only writer of its own file.

## Flashcards

- **Added** filter next to the deck: *Any time / Today / Last 7 days / Last 30 days / Last 90 days* — "review this week's words". The due badge follows it, and the page says plainly when nothing was added in that window.
- **Direction** switch: *RU → EN* shows the Russian and asks for the English; *EN → RU* shows the English and asks you to recall the Russian, revealing the word, its pronunciation and the speaker. One switch, no setup. A word keeps one schedule whichever way it's asked.

## Verified

The whole web flow ran headless in Chromium against an in-page stand-in for Firebase with the same API: a word added; list created, renamed, deleted keeping its word; Direction flipped (English front, Russian back); Added=Today narrowing the session to the word added today and the badge to 1; a teacher creating a code; a student linking with it; the teacher opening the student, sending a word into "Furniture" and queuing a removal; the student signing in and receiving both — the word in Saved marked *Furniture · from teacher@…*, the removed word gone, the account saved and the inbox emptied; the teacher's view reflecting it; and the phone layout with no horizontal overflow. The Cloudflare dry run shows the new vendor bundle shipping and 11,574 repo files kept off the site. The Firestore rules were written to match those flows exactly but can't be executed here — the console's Rules Playground can exercise them. Swift can't be compiled in this environment; the iOS code was written for the compiler blind like the rest of the app, with new tests for the settings shape, the sender field, the Saved rule and the Added cutoff (⌘U).
